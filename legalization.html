Document legalization

Legalization confirms the form or signature of an eligible document for official use in the DRC. It does not validate the truth of the document’s contents.

Official confirmation required. Requirements, fees, processing expectations and submission methods can change. Last reviewed 4 August 2026 · Content owner: Consular Section.

Who is eligible?

Eligibility

  • Civil-status records intended for use in the DRC
  • Academic diplomas and certificates
  • Powers of attorney and notarial acts
  • Eligible commercial or organizational documents

What is required?

Documents

  • Original document to be legalized
  • Clear photocopy of the complete document
  • Prior notarization, certification or apostille when required
  • Copy of the applicant’s identification
  • Translation when required for official use
  • Tracked return envelope for approved mail submissions

What does it cost?

Fee verification

Fees can vary by document type. Confirm the current per-document amount and accepted payment method before submitting.

How long does it take?

Processing guidance

Processing depends on document type, prior authentication and completeness. The Consular Section will confirm current expectations.

How do I submit?

Submission steps

  • Identify the exact document and intended use
  • Complete any required prior state or federal authentication
  • Confirm whether translation is required
  • Verify the fee and submission channel
  • Submit originals only through the approved process

What happens next?

After submission

Check the legalized document when returned and keep copies of the complete submission and receipt.

Verified next step

Confirm before you act.

Do not email passport scans, banking details or other sensitive information unless the Embassy provides an approved secure method.
