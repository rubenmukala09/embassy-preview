About the Embassy

What the Embassy does

The Embassy represents the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the Government of the United States. It provides consular services to Congolese nationals across the country and issues visas to travelers to the DRC.

Its work covers political and diplomatic relations, trade and investment, press and public diplomacy, and consular services: passports, visas, travel documents and the legalization of documents.

Presidency of the Republic

The Ambassador · Head of mission

H.E. Yvette Kapinga Ngandu

Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the United States

Her Excellency Yvette Kapinga Ngandu took up her post in Washington on October 30, 2025. As the personal representative of the President of the Republic, she leads the Embassy’s diplomatic, consular and economic engagement with the Government of the United States.

A career diplomat with more than 25 years of experience across six international organizations, she most recently served as Commissioner for Gender Promotion, Human and Social Development at the Economic Community of Central African States (ECCAS). At the African Union she established and led the Continental Mediation Unit and the Secretariat of the Panel of the Wise, dedicating over a decade to preventive diplomacy across the continent.

She holds a Master’s degree in public administration and international relations from Bowling Green State University in Ohio, and is fluent in French, English, Spanish and Lingala.

Organization

Embassy offices

The Embassy is organized in four offices.

  • Office of the Ambassador

    Directs the mission and represents the DRC before U.S. institutions. The Deputy Chief of Mission coordinates the Embassy’s daily operations.

  • Consular Section

    Passports, visas, travel documents and legalization, and assistance to Congolese nationals across the United States.

  • Economic and Trade Section

    Trade, investment and commercial cooperation between the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the United States.

  • Political Affairs, Press and Communication

    Political dialogue, public diplomacy, media inquiries and the Embassy’s official statements.

All offices are reached through the Embassy’s main email address: info@ambadrcusa.org

History

The DRC and the United States

Selected dates in the history of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and its relations with the United States.

  1. 1960

    Independence and diplomatic relations

    The Congo becomes independent on June 30, 1960. The United States establishes diplomatic relations with the new state the same year.

  2. 1971

    Republic of Zaire

    On October 27, 1971, the country is renamed the Republic of Zaire.

  3. 1997

    Democratic Republic of the Congo

    In May 1997 the country resumes the name Democratic Republic of the Congo.

  4. 2006

    Constitution and general elections

    The current Constitution is promulgated on February 18, 2006, and general elections are held the same year.

  5. 2019

    Peaceful transfer of power

    On January 24, 2019, Félix-⁠Antoine Tshisekedi Tshilombo is sworn in as President, the country’s first peaceful transfer of power since independence.

  6. 2025

    The Washington Agreements

    The DRC–⁠Rwanda Peace Agreement is signed in Washington on June 27, 2025. On December 4, 2025, the Presidents of the DRC and Rwanda sign the Washington Accords for Peace and Prosperity, and the DRC and the United States sign a strategic partnership agreement.
