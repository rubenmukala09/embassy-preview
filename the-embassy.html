About the Embassy

What the Embassy does

The Embassy of the Democratic Republic of the Congo in Washington, D.C. represents the interests of the DRC in the United States. It provides diplomatic engagement, consular services and support for Congolese nationals, visitors, investors and the diaspora.

Representing the DRC to the Government of the United States, Embassy staff engage interested stakeholders on a range of diplomatic, security and economic issues related to the DRC and the United States, while providing consular assistance to Congolese nationals, investors and those seeking to travel to the DRC.

Its work covers political and diplomatic relations, trade and investment, press and public diplomacy, and consular services: visas for all travelers, including foreign applicants; passports and travel documents for Congolese nationals; the legalization of documents; and powers of attorney.

The DRC Government is committed to facilitating the travel of Congolese nationals living abroad. In the United States, the Embassy assists them with the issuance and renewal of DRC passports and with the Tenant-Lieu travel document.

Message from the Ambassador

Welcome to the DRC Embassy in Washington, D.C.

As the Ambassador, I am honored by the opportunity and proud to represent our Government in the United States of America. In this role, I have the responsibility of advancing the Democratic Republic of the Congo’s strategic partnership with the Government of the United States of America and of ensuring our citizens’ interests. While committed to strengthening our bilateral relations, I serve as the primary liaison between the United States and the DRC Government in Kinshasa.

From its nation to its natural resources, the DRC is a rich country that has progressively worked to advance democratic values and has become an emerging market economy with immense potential for foreign direct investment and diaspora engagement in agriculture, energy, infrastructure and tourism. These are the hallmarks of a nation that aims to prosper and to build a stronger future along with its regional partners, and to increase development and investment in the DRC. We are part of the promise of intra-African trade, and we continue to develop the DRC to uplift the 86 million Congolese people.

Based in Washington, D.C., and traveling regularly throughout the United States, on behalf of the DRC Government and the Embassy team, I welcome you to the Embassy’s website.

Sincerely,

H.E. Yvette Kapinga Ngandu

Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the United States

As the personal representative of the President of the Republic, she leads the Embassy’s diplomatic, consular and economic engagement with the Government of the United States.

Career

From August 30, 2020 to August 2025, she was Commissioner for Gender Promotion, Human and Social Development at the Commission of the Economic Community of Central African States (ECCAS), with Libreville, Gabon, as her duty station. She was the first woman to head the largest department of ECCAS, overseeing ten key sectors: health, humanitarian action, education and scientific research, culture, migration, employment, gender, youth, sports and social affairs. At 44, she was the youngest of the seven elected members of the new Commission.

She has more than 25 years of professional experience in the diplomatic world, with strong expertise in mediation and in conflict resolution and prevention, gained in six international organizations and diplomatic bodies such as ECCAS; the African Peer Review Mechanism (APRM) in Johannesburg; the African Union Commission in Addis Ababa, where she served for twelve years; the United Nations Secretariat and the International Peace Institute (IPI) in New York; and the National Endowment for Democracy (NED), Voice of America and the U.S. Department of State in Washington, D.C.

From 2003 to 2008 she was a senior Africa program manager at the International Peace Institute in New York. Before joining IPI, she worked in Washington, D.C. with the National Endowment for Democracy, Voice of America and the U.S. Department of State.

At the African Union

Her most notable achievements at continental and regional level include contributing to the AU Declaration on Gender, adopted at the Conference of Heads of State in July 2004, and to the AU Report on Women and Children in Armed Conflict (2010), which led to a decision by the AU Conference (Summit) of Heads of State and Government in June 2017 to establish a Pan-African Network of Women Mediators, FemWise-Africa.

In 2008 she created, and went on to lead, the African Union Continental Mediation Unit and the Secretariat of the Panel of the Wise. As coordinator of both, she managed the AU’s preventive diplomacy and mediation operations for 12 years, coordinating the conflict-prevention, mediation and good-offices missions of the members of the Panel of the Wise.

Having taken part in more than 30 African Union summits of heads of state and organized countless seminars and peace missions in African countries, she is well versed in African affairs.

Thematic reports she supervised

She supervised and coordinated thematic reports that enabled the successive Chairpersons of the AU Commission (Mr. Jean Ping, Ms. Dlamini Zuma and Mr. Mahamat Moussa Faki) and AU member states to take appropriate action on emerging threats to peace and security in Africa:

  • Report on managing unconstitutional changes of government (2012)

Organization

Embassy divisions

The Embassy is organized in four divisions. Each one can be reached through the Embassy’s main contact details.

  • Ambassador’s Office

    The Ambassador’s Office leads the Embassy and represents the Government of the DRC in the United States. The Ambassador is responsible for strengthening U.S.–Africa bilateral relations. The Deputy Chief of Mission coordinates the Embassy’s daily operations.

    Meeting requests. To request a meeting with the Ambassador, email the Embassy with the complete details of your request. The Office reviews every request and responds accordingly; because of the high volume of requests, it may not respond to every one. For consular questions, contact the Consular Affairs division directly.

    Email a meeting request

  • Consular Affairs

    Visa · Passport · Tenant-Lieu

    The Consular Affairs division provides consular services to Congolese nationals living in the United States and issues visas to U.S. citizens and to any foreign national seeking to visit the DRC. It handles passports, the Tenant-Lieu travel document, the legalization of documents and powers of attorney.

    Download the visa, passport or Tenant-Lieu application from the Consular Services page. Completed applications can be mailed to the Embassy or dropped off at the Embassy. Biometric passport applications now begin with online pre-registration: follow the current passport steps. For questions about consular affairs or other matters, or to arrange an appointment, contact the division by email or telephone; there are no online bookings until further notice.

    Consular Services Biometric passport steps Email a consular question

  • Economic and Trade Section

    Investment information

    Trade, investment and commercial cooperation between the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the United States. Interested in investing in the DRC? Contact the Embassy to learn more about the various investment opportunities: the Embassy is your door to the DRC.

    The DRC’s investment agency, the National Agency for Investment Promotion (ANAPI), provides investment facilitation services for initial investments over $200,000. It is mandated to simplify the investment process, make procedures more transparent, assist new foreign investors and improve the image of the DRC as an investment destination.

    Current investment regulations prohibit foreign investors from engaging in informal small retail commerce (“petit commerce”) and restrict foreign majority ownership in the agricultural industry.

    When you write, please describe your investment interest.

    Political dialogue, public diplomacy, media inquiries and the Embassy’s official statements.

History

The DRC and the United States

Selected dates in the history of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and its relations with the United States.

  1. 1960

    Independence and diplomatic relations

    The Congo becomes independent on June 30, 1960. The United States establishes diplomatic relations with the new state the same year.

  2. 1971

    Republic of Zaire

    On October 27, 1971, the country is renamed the Republic of Zaire.

  3. 1997

    Democratic Republic of the Congo

    In May 1997 the country resumes the name Democratic Republic of the Congo.

  4. 2006

    Constitution and general elections

    The current Constitution is promulgated on February 18, 2006, and general elections are held the same year.

  5. 2019

    Peaceful transfer of power

    On January 24, 2019, Félix-⁠Antoine Tshisekedi Tshilombo is sworn in as President, the country’s first peaceful transfer of power since independence.

  6. 2025

    The Washington Agreements

    The DRC–⁠Rwanda Peace Agreement is signed in Washington on June 27, 2025. On December 4, 2025, the Presidents of the DRC and Rwanda sign the Washington Accords for Peace and Prosperity, and the DRC and the United States sign a strategic partnership agreement.
