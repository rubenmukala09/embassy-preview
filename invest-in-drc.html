Why the DRC

Resources are only the beginning

The Democratic Republic of the Congo offers extensive farmland, mineral wealth, energy resources and a strategic position in Central Africa. The opportunity is to create more value inside the country through responsible production, infrastructure, services and skilled employment.

Sectors

Priority investment sectors

The Embassy publishes information on ten priority sectors. Each link opens the Embassy’s full sector page in a new tab.

  • Agriculture & agribusiness

    Arable land, diverse growing regions and value-chain opportunities across crops, fisheries, inputs, equipment, processing and agricultural logistics.

  • Banking & finance

    Commercial banking, microfinance, insurance, leasing, electronic money and broader financial inclusion for households and enterprises.

  • Energy

    Hydroelectricity, transmission, distributed power, solar, biomass, wind and geothermal opportunities serving communities and industry.

  • Forestry

    Sustainable forest management, conservation-linked enterprise, certified timber value chains and local processing with environmental safeguards.

  • Housing & urban development

    Affordable and workforce housing, planned communities, construction materials, water, sanitation and resilient urban infrastructure.

  • Hydrocarbons

    Exploration, production, storage, transport and downstream services governed by the sector’s current legal and environmental framework.

  • Infrastructure

    Transport corridors, logistics, public works, water systems and infrastructure that connects production centres to cities and regional markets.

  • Mining & value chains

    Responsible extraction, mineral processing, traceability, equipment, energy and local supplier development around strategic resources.

  • Tourism & hospitality

    Conservation-led travel, hospitality, aviation, cultural experiences and destination services connected to the DRC’s natural heritage.

  • Transportation & logistics

    Road, rail, river, lake, maritime and aviation systems for a country whose scale makes integrated mobility essential.

Investor support

How ANAPI supports investors

ANAPI, the National Agency for Investment Promotion, is the principal investor-facilitation body identified by the Embassy. Its support includes:

  • Orientation and company-establishment guidance
  • Access to investment-code procedures
  • Sector contacts and partnership development
  • Land and premises searches
  • Coordination with the relevant authorities
Visit the ANAPI website

A typical pathway from inquiry to decision

  1. Define the project

    Clarify the sector, site, capital, partners, employment plan and the value the project will create inside the DRC.

  2. Engage ANAPI

    Request current guidance, confirm the appropriate legal structure and identify sector-specific authorizations.

  3. Prepare the file

    Develop the business plan, legal documents, equipment schedule and evidence required for the applicable review.

  4. Verify approvals

    Confirm current taxes, incentives, licences, land rights, labour rules and environmental obligations before committing funds.

Legal framework

Investment code, protections and incentives

Principles described in the Embassy’s published guidance. This is orientation only, not legal, tax or financial advice.

Investor protections

  • Equal treatment of national and foreign investors
  • Transfer of qualifying investment income abroad
  • Amicable settlement or arbitration pathways
  • DRC participation in OHADA, ICSID and MIGA frameworks

Eligibility conditions

  • Operate as an entity under Congolese law
  • Meet the applicable project-cost threshold
  • Comply with environmental and labour rules
  • Demonstrate the required local value-added rate
Tax and customs incentives

The Embassy’s published summary lists possible exemptions on qualifying machinery, materials and equipment; profits; property tax; and certain company-formation duties for approved projects. It describes benefit periods linked to economic regions. Investors must obtain current, project-specific confirmation from ANAPI and the competent tax and customs authorities.

Read the Embassy tax summary

Investment code and approval procedure

The Embassy describes an ANAPI review followed by consideration through the approval process and an inter-ministerial decision. Required materials may include a project file, equipment list, proof of the company’s legal existence and filing-fee evidence. Confirm the current procedure before preparing or paying for an application.
