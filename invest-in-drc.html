Why the DRC

Land, resources and regional access

The Democratic Republic of the Congo boasts some of the most abundant natural resources on the planet. With a strong economy, rich farmland and bountiful mineral wealth, the DRC is progressively building towards a brighter future.

Its energy resources and strategic position in Central Africa add to that potential. The opportunity is to create more value inside the country through responsible production, infrastructure, services and skilled employment.

Sectors

Priority investment sectors

The Government is focusing on these sectors to grow the economy and create jobs for the Congolese people. The DRC is known for its mining and plans to diversify its investment areas, including but not limited to the following.

  • Agriculture & agribusiness

    The DRC has more available farmland than any other country in Africa, with the agricultural potential to feed close to two billion people. Of an estimated 80 million hectares of arable land, 10% is currently in use. Agriculture contributes 18% of GDP and accounts for over 60% of new jobs.

  • Banking & finance

    The banking, insurance and microfinance sectors aim to create specialized financial institutions and make existing banks more efficient. The system comprises the Central Bank of the Congo (BCC), 18 commercial banks, savings and credit cooperatives, microfinance institutions, money-transfer services, one development bank (SOFIDE) and a postal checking system. Citibank-DRC, a wholly owned subsidiary of Citigroup, is the only U.S. bank in the DRC.

  • Energy

    Non-renewable resources such as oil, natural gas and uranium, and renewable sources including hydroelectric, biomass, solar, wind and geothermal power. The Congo River, the second-largest river in the world, offers an estimated 100,000 MW across 780 sites in 145 territories and 76,000 villages: about 37% of Africa’s potential and 6% of the world’s. The Government aims to raise the level of service to 32% by 2030.

  • Forestry

    The Congo Basin covers an estimated 530 million hectares, 200–300 million of them forest or woodland: more than 45% of Africa’s equatorial forest and 6% of the world’s tropical reserves. Congolese forest holds some 1,000 tree species and 11,000 plant species, many described as medicinal; in its densest parts only 1% of sunlight reaches the ground. Investment projects set a target of planting 3 million hectares of forest by 2025. Unique to the DRC: lianas up to 914 meters (3,000 feet) long and teak trees up to 50 meters high.

  • Housing & urban development

    The Government’s vision is to reorganize the housing sector through institutional reform and capacity building, improve housing through land development policy and the supervision of real estate, and raise funds to eliminate the housing shortage, especially in poor urban areas. In 2011, the Cité Kin Oasis project completed 1,000 social houses in Kinshasa (Bandalungwa).

  • Hydrocarbons

    Oil and gas discoveries in the east give the DRC the second-largest crude oil reserves in Central and Southern Africa, mainly in the four major lakes bordering Tanzania, Burundi, Rwanda and Uganda. Proven reserves stand at 180 million barrels, and total petroleum reserves are estimated at over 5 billion barrels. Production is currently limited to the Coast Basin: 25,000 barrels per day offshore, all exported.

  • Infrastructure

    Numerous construction opportunities for American firms, most structured as public-private partnerships. In March 2016, the Ministry of Infrastructure and Public Works identified over $6 billion in transportation and utility construction and rehabilitation projects for the following six years. The Government aims to modernize railroad infrastructure and airport safety and security equipment, and to maintain, lay out and mark the country’s waterways.

  • Mining & value chains

    The sector has attracted subsidiaries of large multinationals, including Tenke Fungurume Mining (Freeport-McMoRan), Kolwezi Copper Company (Kinross-EGMF), Kibali Gold Mining, Mutanda, Ashanti Goldfields Kilo and Twangiza Mining. The DRC’s mining potential represents 1,100 different minerals, and every province has its own particular minerals.

  • Tourism & hospitality

    Myriad opportunities in rehabilitating and building hotels, restaurants and other tourism infrastructure. Wildlife reserves, indigenous cultures and geological wonders make the DRC ideal for those who seek to connect with nature and mankind. Protected areas cover 12% of the country, including 7 national parks and 57 reserves with great ecotourism potential, and attractions range from the seaside to safaris and cultural sites, for business and leisure travelers alike. The DRC has four endemic species (mountain gorillas, okapi, bonobos and the Congolese peacock) and ecosystems covering almost 145 million hectares.

  • Transportation & logistics

    The country’s transportation networks offer immense investment opportunities. The Government plans a new public transport policy to attract private investment, improve urban roads, strengthen public carriers’ rolling stock and spare parts with around 1,500 buses over five years (300 a year), and extend the Kinshasa–Matadi railway to Banana for the deep-water port.

Investor support

How ANAPI supports investors

The DRC’s investment agency, the National Agency for Investment Promotion (ANAPI), provides investment facilitation services for initial investments over US $200,000. It is mandated to simplify the investment process, make procedures more transparent, assist new foreign investors and improve the image of the DRC as an investment destination.

  • Help with setting up a company
  • Tax and customs benefits under the Investment Code
  • Entry or settlement visas
  • Land and premises, with water and electricity
  • Partnerships with public and private operators
  • Access to finance and sector licenses
  • Welcome, appointments and accompaniment in Kinshasa and the provinces

Approval for Investment Code benefits

  1. Submit the file to ANAPI

    The investment project, the list of equipment to import, proof of the company’s legal existence and proof that the filing fee has been paid to the bank: US $1,000 for large companies, US $500 for SMEs.

  2. ANAPI analyzes the project

    ANAPI reviews the project and gives its opinion on its approval under the Investment Code.

  3. The Approval Council decides

    The Approval Council validates or rejects the project, and ANAPI informs the promoter.

  4. Decree within 30 days

    The inter-ministerial decree, signed by the Ministers of Planning and Finance, is handed over within 30 days at most. After that deadline, approval is deemed granted.

Legal framework

Laws, legislation and taxes

Summaries of the legal texts the Embassy publishes for investors. This is orientation only, not legal, tax or financial advice.

  • Laws

    The Investment Code facilitates and secures investments that promote the development of the country, and simplifies the approval procedure for customs and tax benefits. Read about support structures for investors, the approval procedure for Investment Code benefits and investment protection mechanisms.

    Read the page

  • Legislation

    In 2002, new laws strengthened the Central Bank’s role as regulator and supervisor and increased its independence. Act No. 11/020 of September 15, 2011 governs microfinance, and Act No. 15/005 of March 17, 2015 liberalized insurance and established the Insurance Regulatory and Control Authority (ARCA). Texts for every sector, from agriculture to transportation.

    Read the page

  • Taxes related to investment

    Projects approved through ANAPI are exempt from duties and taxes on imported machinery, materials and equipment (except a 2% administrative fee and VAT, which is reimbursed), from tax on profits, from property tax and from the ad valorem duty on forming an LLC or increasing its capital, for 3 to 5 years depending on the region.

Investor protections

  • Equal treatment of national and foreign investors
  • No nationalization or expropriation, except in extreme cases for a public purpose, with fair compensation paid in advance
  • Free transfer abroad of revenues, dividends and other investment earnings
  • Amicable settlement or arbitration of disputes; membership of ICSID, MIGA, ACA and OHADA
  • Bilateral investment agreements with partners including the United States

Eligibility for Investment Code benefits

  • Be an economic entity under Congolese law
  • Invest at least US $200,000 in total, or US $10,000 for SMEs and SMIs
  • Commit to complying with environmental and labor regulations
  • Ensure a value-added rate of at least 35%

Foreign investors and staff

  • Foreign investors may not engage in informal small retail trade, known as “petit commerce.”
  • Foreign majority ownership in the agricultural industry is restricted.
  • Visas for foreign workers are limited to six consecutive months. Under the Embassy’s current consular fee schedule, a six-month visa costs US $400 (single entry) or US $450 (multiple entries).
  • After the initial temporary work visa, which is normally not difficult to obtain, a foreign worker may qualify for a more expensive establishment visa valid for at least one year.
  • To encourage local employment, salaries paid to expatriates are taxed at a higher rate than those of local staff.
