Agriculture in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The DRC has more available farmland than any other country in Africa, with the agricultural potential to feed close to two billion people. Of an estimated 80 million hectares of arable land, about 10% is currently in use. Agriculture contributes 18% of GDP and accounts for more than 60% of new jobs.

Overview

Arable land
About 80 million hectares, of which about 10% is in use
Share of the economy
18% of GDP and more than 60% of new jobs
Rainfall
A little over 58 inches (147 cm) a year on average
Climate
Monthly average of 23–27 °C across 90% of the country; the dry season lasts less than four months, except in the far south
Forest
The second-largest rainforest in the world, after the Amazon
Main crops
Cassava, plantains, maize, groundnuts, tobacco, coffee, sugar cane, cocoa and rice; rubber from rubber trees and palm oil from palm kernels
Soils
Ferralsols, sandy clay soils, sandy soils, recent volcanic soils, alluvial plain soils, soils on ancient rock, arenoferrals, hydrokaolisols and others

Leading sub-sectors

  • Crop planting and cultivation
  • Fisheries, land and wildlife conservation
  • Fertilizers, herbicides, pesticides and fungicides
  • Farm equipment leasing and financing
  • Agricultural industrial parks

Government goals

The Government’s goals include, among others:

  • Increase agriculture’s contribution to economic growth.
  • Restore the country’s food security.
  • Reduce poverty and insecurity in rural areas.
  • Increase the production of food and sustainable products.
  • Encourage import substitution.

Securing land for agriculture

The right to use land is called a concession. It is legally established by a land registration certificate granted by the State and becomes unassailable two years after it is established.

Perpetual concession
Reserved for Congolese nationals, with no time limit
Ordinary concession
Open to Congolese and foreign nationals, for 25 years, renewable without limit

Steps to obtain an ordinary concession for agricultural use

  1. Identify the land

    Choose the plot you intend to farm.

  2. Agree with the traditional authority

    Sign an agreement with the traditional authority of the area, against payment of a sum of money and goods in kind according to custom.

  3. Vacancy inquiry and demarcation

    The local departments of the Ministry of Agriculture and the Ministry of Land Affairs confirm that the land is vacant and mark its boundaries.

  4. Sign the concession contract

    The contract is signed with the Registrar of Real Estate Titles.

  5. Registration certificate

    The Registrar of Real Estate Titles issues the registration certificate.

Who approves the concession

The approving authority depends on the size of the land:

Area of the landApproval
2,000 ha or moreApproved by a law
1,000 ha to 2,000 haApproved by order of the President of the Republic
Over 200 ha, up to 1,000 haApproved by the Ministry of Land Affairs
200 ha or lessSigned by the Governor of the Province (who may delegate to the Registrar of Real Estate Titles for land under 50 ha)

For land applications, contact the Ministry of Land Affairs or the National Agency for Investment Promotion (ANAPI): anapi.cd

Legal framework

Investment Code (Act No. 004/2002 of February 21, 2002)

The Investment Code aims to:

  1. Promote civil engineering companies that build and maintain roads and highways, and the movement of people and goods by land, river or air.
  2. Promote investment that develops agriculture and agribusiness through mechanization, to ensure food self-sufficiency, reduce commodity imports, raise rural incomes, supply food industries with raw materials and enlarge the domestic market for consumer goods.
  3. Promote heavy investment to establish a strong industrial base that supports sustainable economic growth.
  4. Encourage investment that processes national natural resources on site, to increase added value and export volumes.

Decree No. 13/049 of June 10, 2014

This decree sets the tax regime for companies eligible for the Strategic Partnership along the value chain. It creates a development tax system within the legal framework for economic promotion and the revival of national industrial units likely to improve living conditions in national communities. It helps the Government and the private sector to direct, organize and carry out partnership investment programs in areas and sectors with strong integration potential, so that a large part of the population can take part in economic and social activity in well-defined geographical areas.

Agricultural Code (Law No. 11/022 of December 24, 2011)

The law on the fundamental principles relating to agriculture aims to:

  1. Promote the sustainable development of agricultural potential and land, integrating social and environmental aspects.
  2. Boost agricultural production through a special customs and tax regime, notably to achieve food self-sufficiency.
  3. Boost exports of agricultural products to generate significant resources for investment.
  4. Promote local industries that process agricultural products.
  5. Introduce new renewable energy technologies.
  6. Involve the provinces, decentralized territorial entities and farmers in promoting and carrying out agricultural development.

Investment incentives under the Agricultural Code

  • The cost of building a road from a main road to the farm is tax deductible.
  • Farmers receive discounts on water and energy prices (electricity and petroleum).
  • Water and energy produced by a farm for its own use are tax exempt.
  • Industrial farmers may set aside a tax-free provision of up to 3% of turnover for land rehabilitation, major risks and agricultural disasters; it is added back to taxable income if unused after two years.
  • Farming inputs are exempt from import and export duties.
  • Land used exclusively for agriculture is exempt from property tax.
  • Vehicles used exclusively for agricultural operations are tax free.
  • All administrative fees payable to government services at the border together may not exceed 0.25% of the value of exported goods.
