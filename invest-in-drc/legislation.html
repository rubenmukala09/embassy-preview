Investment legislation by sector in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The main laws, codes, decrees and orders that govern investment in each sector of the economy of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, as summarized by the Embassy.

Banking, microfinance and insurance

In 2002, the Government passed new laws to strengthen the Central Bank’s role as regulatory and supervisory authority and to increase its independence. To promote mass banking, financial inclusion and a sound financial system, the Central Bank also defined a specific legal framework for microfinance.

Act No. 11/020 of September 15, 2011

Defines the rules governing microfinance activity.

Act No. 15/005 of March 17, 2015 (Insurance Code)

This insurance legislation was adopted to liberalize the insurance sector and attract private insurance companies. It led to the creation and effective establishment of the Insurance Regulatory and Control Authority (ARCA – Autorité de Régulation et de Contrôle des Assurances).

Agriculture

Investment Code (Act No. 004/2002 of February 21, 2002)

The new Investment Code has the following objectives:

  1. Promote the establishment of civil engineering companies responsible for building and maintaining roads and highways, and the movement of people and goods by land, river or air.
  2. Promote investment that develops agriculture and agribusiness through mechanization, to ensure food self-sufficiency and so reduce commodity imports, raise rural incomes, supply food industries with raw materials and enlarge the domestic market for consumer goods.
  3. Promote heavy investment to establish a strong industrial base that supports sustainable economic growth.
  4. Encourage investment that develops national natural resources on site, to increase added value and export volumes.

Decree No. 13/049 of June 10, 2014

This decree sets the tax regime that applies to companies eligible for the Strategic Partnership along the value chain. It establishes a development tax system as part of the legal framework for economic promotion and for reviving national industrial units likely to improve the living conditions of national communities.

It is an instrument of economic promotion that helps the Government and the private sector to direct, organize and carry out partnership investment programs in areas and sectors with strong integration potential, so that a large part of the population can take part in economic and social activity in well-defined geographical areas.

Agricultural Code (Law No. 11/022 of December 24, 2011)

The law on the fundamental principles relating to agriculture aims to:

  1. Promote the sustainable development of agricultural potential and land, integrating social and environmental aspects.
  2. Boost agricultural production by establishing a special customs and tax regime, in order to achieve food self-sufficiency, among other goals.
  3. Boost exports of agricultural products to generate significant resources for investment.
  4. Promote local industries that process agricultural products.
  5. Introduce new renewable energy technologies.
  6. Involve the province, the decentralized territorial entity and the farmer in promoting and implementing agricultural development.

Key investment provisions of the Agricultural Code

  • Expenses incurred by investors to build a road connecting their farm to a main road are tax deductible.
  • Farmers receive discounts on water and energy prices (electricity and petroleum).
  • Water and energy produced by a farm for its own consumption are exempt from tax.
  • Industrial farmers may set aside a non-taxable provision of up to 3% of their turnover for land rehabilitation, major risks and agricultural disasters. The provision is added back to the taxable base if it is not used within two years.
  • Farming inputs are exempt from import and export duties.
  • Land used exclusively for agriculture is exempt from property tax.
  • Vehicles used exclusively for agricultural operations are tax free.
  • All administrative fees payable to government services at the border, combined, may not exceed 0.25% of the value of exported goods.

Energy

Law No. 14/011 of June 17, 2014 (electricity sector)

This law regulates the energy sector in the DRC. It provides for:

  • The effective liberalization of the sector.
  • The promotion and harmonious development of supply in urban, peri-urban and rural areas.
  • Coverage of the electricity needs of all categories of users through quality supply.
  • A guarantee of fair competition between operators and of users’ rights.

The law applies to the production, transmission, distribution, import, export and marketing of electricity by any operator.

More about the energy sector

Forestry

Forest Law (Law No. 011/2002 of August 29, 2002) and Decree No. 05/116 of October 24, 2005

This law governs the exploitation of forest resources throughout the country. It promotes the rational and sustainable management of natural forest resources, to increase their contribution to the economic, social and cultural development of present generations while preserving forest ecosystems and biodiversity for future generations.

More about the forestry sector

Housing and land

Land Law No. 73-021 of July 20, 1973

The law on the general regime of property, land tenure, real estate and security interests sets precise standards for the acquisition, sale and leasing of land and concessions. It frames the decisions of sellers, lessees and buyers on the procedure for every transaction leading to the issue, by the Congolese State, of highly secure real estate titles.

Bakajika Law of June 7, 1966

This law states that “the soil and subsoil belong to the State.” It was intended to bring order to the land sector.

Ministerial Order No. Cab/MINA/TUHITPR/007/2013 of June 26, 2013 (building permits)

This order regulates the granting of building permits in the DRC. Anyone wishing to undertake a property development, urban project, building or structure of any kind in durable materials and in accordance with good building practice, anywhere in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, must first obtain a building permit from the relevant urban planning and housing administration.

Circular Note No. 005/CAB/MIN/AFF FUNC/2013 of June 12, 2013

Sets the procedure and time limits for transferring land and property rights.

Hydrocarbons

The former Mines and Hydrocarbons Law

Until recently, the DRC oil and gas sector was governed by Ordinance-Law No. 81-013 establishing general legislation on mines and hydrocarbons, as amended by Ordinance-Law No. 82-039 of November 5, 1982 and Law No. 86-008 of December 27, 1986 (the Mines and Hydrocarbons Law). Since 2002, however, the mining sector has been governed by a separate Mining Code, which made many provisions of the Mines and Hydrocarbons Law obsolete.

Hydrocarbons Law No. 15/012 of August 1, 2015 and Decree No. 16/010 of April 19, 2016

After several years of deliberation on reforming oil and gas legislation, the DRC adopted Law No. 15/012 of August 1, 2015 establishing a general regime for hydrocarbons (the Hydrocarbons Law). The reform was completed by Decree No. 16/010 of April 19, 2016 establishing the hydrocarbons regulations (the Regulations), which among other things adds detail to the applicable fiscal regime. The main provisions of the Hydrocarbons Law and Regulations relevant to oil companies engaged in upstream activities are summarized below.

State ownership
The State owns all national hydrocarbon resources up to the point of export.
Award of rights
Rights to carry out petroleum exploration or exploitation operations may be granted to local or foreign legal entities selected through competitive bidding, on technical and financial criteria set by the Minister of Hydrocarbons and approved by the Council of Ministers. The rights are granted under a petroleum contract. Unlike the former Mines and Hydrocarbons Law, the Hydrocarbons Law no longer provides for concessions.
Local subsidiary
Foreign entities must form local subsidiaries under DRC law to engage in petroleum exploration or exploitation activities.
Exploration rights
Valid for 3 years (4 years for areas with difficult geological or access conditions), renewable twice for the same period. Under the Mines and Hydrocarbons Law, the maximum duration of exploration rights was 8 years.
Exploitation rights
Granted for a maximum of 20 years, renewable once for a maximum of 10 years (comparable to concessions under the Mines and Hydrocarbons Law). Holders must begin work within 12 months of the approval of the development and production plan.
State participation
The State-owned oil company (presumably COHYDRO, Congolaise des Hydrocarbures, created by Decree-Law No. 245 of August 9, 1999) is entitled to a minimum 20% stake in upstream petroleum activities, under an association contract with third parties that does not create a separate legal entity.

Infrastructure

Law No. 14/011 of June 17, 2014 (liberalization of the energy sector)

This law opens the energy sector to private operators. It applies to production, transmission, distribution, import, export and marketing. It does not apply to plants with an installed capacity of 50 kW or less, to non-commercial use, to signal or speech distribution facilities, to scientific research facilities, or to installations for State security.

Public-private partnership bill

According to the Embassy’s published information, a bill on public-private partnerships was awaiting consideration by the National Assembly. It provides for forms such as service contracts, management and maintenance contracts (leasing), rental and construction-management, and build-transfer-management.

Mining

Mining Code (Law No. 007/2002 of July 11, 2002) and Mining Regulations (Decree No. 038/2003 of March 26, 2003)

The current Congolese Mining Code was enacted by the Congolese Congress on July 11, 2002. Its implementing measures are set out in the Mining Regulations of March 26, 2003. The mining sector is thus regulated by Law No. 007/2002 of July 11, 2002 on the Mining Code and Decree No. 038/2003 of March 26, 2003 on the Mining Regulations.

The Code classifies mineral deposits as mines or quarries. The President of the Republic may declassify or reclassify a mining substance as a quarry product, and vice versa.

The Mining Code pursues the following objectives:

  • Create a win-win framework for all.
  • Develop and encourage infrastructure for all.
  • Develop the DRC’s human capital.
  • Develop local processing capacity.
  • Develop the DRC as a destination for foreign capital and technology.
  • Encourage the immediate development of the country, not speculation.

Revision of the Mining Code

As described in the Embassy’s published information, the new Mining Code aims in particular to:

  1. Strengthen the Government’s control over the mining sector.
  2. Further regulate the social and environmental responsibility of mining companies.
  3. Incorporate the latest changes in the Congolese administrative context, such as the introduction of VAT into the Congolese tax regime.
  4. Address financial and accounting practices, such as high interest rates charged by operators’ affiliated companies acting as lenders, used by mining companies to channel profits to their foreign shareholders’ groups.

Ten-year stability guarantee

The current Mining Code provides a 10-year stability guarantee: the rights attached to mining rights that are valid on the date the act amending the Mining Code is promulgated remain unchanged and vested for 10 years. This 10-year period starts either:

  1. From the entry into force of the act amending the Mining Code, for mining rights that are valid at that time; or
  2. From the date a mining right is granted, provided it was granted under a valid research permit that existed when the act amending the Mining Code entered into force.

New provinces

From a political point of view, the redefinition of the DRC’s provinces is also likely to affect operators. Since July 2015, when the Government gave effect to the increase in the number of provinces from 11 to 26 mandated by the 2006 Constitution, the main copper-producing region, Katanga, has been split into four provinces. The 400-kilometer (249-mile) journey from Kolwezi to the Zambian border now crosses two provinces, Haut-Katanga and Lualaba, instead of one.

OHADA and the one-stop shop

At the regional level, the DRC joined the Organization for the Harmonization of Business Law in Africa (OHADA) in July 2012. OHADA law is of particular interest to mining companies, as it mainly covers commercial, corporate, loan-guarantee, accounting and arbitration law. It entered into force in the DRC on September 12, 2012. In addition, a one-stop shop for business start-ups (Guichet unique) has been instituted and shows encouraging development.

Mining institutions

The Mining Cadastre receives applications for mining rights, grants mining rights and keeps the records of mining rights, among other functions. The DRC has also created a national committee for the transparency initiative in the management of the extractive industries. Regulations are issued by the Ministry of Mines, which supervises mining activities at national level.

Transportation

Road transport

Law No. 78-022 of August 30, 1978 on the new Highway Code, in force since July 25, 1979, regulates traffic on public roads by pedestrians, vehicles and draft, pack or saddle animals and livestock.

Rail transport

The railway sector is a State monopoly in the DRC. The decree of the Sovereign King of October 10, 1908 on railway policing, which coordinated earlier measures on the railway police, as amended by Orders No. 82/TP of September 18, 1928 and No. 41/06 of February 13, 1954 on the same subject, is the principal legislation governing railway transport. Railway transport activities are also governed by the International Union of Railways, the Union of African Railways and the public rail transport enterprises themselves.

Air transport

Air transport is governed by Law No. 10/014 of December 31, 2010 on international civil aviation, which aims, among other things, to give the country adequate and efficient aviation legislation.

Maritime transport

Ordinance-Law No. 66-98 of March 14, 1966 establishing the Maritime Navigation Code is the basic legal framework for maritime transport in the DRC.

River and lake transport

Ordinance-Law No. 66-96 of March 14, 1966 establishing the River and Lake Navigation Code is the backbone of the sector’s legislation.

More about the transportation sector

Tourism

The revival of tourism is among the Government’s priorities. The sector, which depends closely on the environment, has been placed under a ministry that is to be provided shortly with framework legislation, which is being drafted.

More about the tourism sector
