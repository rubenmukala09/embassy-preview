Transportation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

Opportunities to invest across the country’s many transportation networks are immense, building on what the Government has already achieved.

Outlook

The Government’s plans for the sector are to:

  • Develop a new public transport policy that combines efficiency, profitability and social goals, with incentives to attract private investment, particularly in mass transit systems such as urban trains, trams and urban river transport;
  • Improve urban roads to reduce vehicle depreciation costs and ease traffic;
  • Increase the vehicle fleet of the State-owned public transport companies;
  • Encourage private operators to invest in the sector through public-private partnerships, particularly in mass transit systems;
  • Strengthen public carriers’ rolling stock and spare parts with around 1,500 buses over 5 years, at a rate of 300 buses a year;
  • Create a national fund to promote public transport, financed by road tolls and some public car parks in major cities;
  • Rehabilitate and modernize the SCTP urban train;
  • Set up an SCTP urban river transport system to ease surface transport;
  • Extend the Kinshasa–Matadi railway to Banana for the planned deep-water port, among other projects.

Railway network: 5,033 km

The lines are not interconnected and do not share the same standards (gauges of 1.067 m, 1 m and 0.6 m):

  • A line linking Matadi to Kinshasa, 365 km long, with a 1.067 m gauge.
  • A wider network connected with Zambia, Angola and Lake Tanganyika, centered on Lubumbashi. It covers 3,641 km (including 858 km electrified) in Katanga, Kasai-Occidental, Kasai-Oriental and Maniema, with a 1.067 m gauge:
    • Katanga line: Kabalo – Kamina – Kolwezi – Likasi – Lubumbashi – Sakania – Zambia
    • Kasai line: Ilebo – Kananga – Mwene Ditu – Kamina
    • Benguela railway: Kamina – Dilolo – Angola
    • Kindu railway: Kongolo – Kabalo – Nyunzu – Kalemie
    • Kindu railway: Kongolo – Kabalo – Kabongo – Kamina
  • A line linking Kisangani to Ubundu, with a 1 m gauge.
  • A narrow-gauge line in the Uele region (0.6 m gauge):
    • Bumba – Aketi – Bondo
    • Bumba – Aketi – Buta – Isiro – Wemba

Waterways and ports

Maritime, river and lake network: 16,238 km

  • The Matadi–Banana maritime reach (150 km);
  • The middle reach: the Congo River between Kinshasa and Kisangani (about 1,700 km), and the Kasai River and its tributaries (11,758 km);
  • The upper reach: the Lualaba and the lakes (2,630 km).

Ports and lake and river routes

  • 40 equipped river and lake ports, including Kinshasa, Ilebo, Kalemie, Kisangani, Mbandaka, Kindu and Ubundu.
  • 3 seaports: Matadi, Boma and Banana.
  • 24 waterways, the main alternative to roads and railways.
  • The Lake Kivu system, 106 km long, linking Bukavu to Goma.
  • Lake Tanganyika, 1,425 km long, linking the DRC to Zambia, Tanzania and Burundi.
  • The river system from Kindu to Ubundu (310 km) and from Kongolo to Malemba Nkulu (390 km).

Air transport

The air network has 270 airport facilities, including five international airports in Kinshasa, Lubumbashi, Kisangani, Goma and Gbadolite.

Achievements

  • Rehabilitation of several airports, including:
    • N’djili Airport in Kinshasa (terminal building, control tower and runways)
    • Goma airport (runway extension)
    • Luano Airport in Lubumbashi (runway extension)
    • Bangboka Airport in Kisangani
  • Creation of the national airline, Congo Airways.
  • Creation of TRANSCO to make up for the shortage of buses in Kinshasa.
  • Elimination of illegal taxes and charges in river and lake transport.
  • Rehabilitation of roads.
  • Vehicle loans granted by the Congolese State to private road transport operators.
  • Rehabilitation of the ITB Kokolo boat, at a cost of US$2.5 million.
  • Reopening of the Benguela railway following the agreement between the Angolan and Congolese States.
  • Grant of equipment: 10 locomotives for SNCC.

Investment procedures

Approval for road and rail transport

The procedure and the file are the same for public road transport and for public rail transport.

  1. Request the licenses

    Apply to the General Secretariat of Transport and Communication Routes for:

    • A transport license
    • A roadworthiness certificate

  2. Apply for approval

    Submit an application for approval to the Minister of Transport and Communication Routes, with a copy to the General Secretariat of Transport and Communication Routes and to the Manager of Land Transport. The file must contain:

    • Notarized articles of association
    • National Identification Number
    • Number in the Trade and Personal Property Credit Register (RCCM)
    • Guarantee instrument
    • Insurance policy
    • Curriculum vitae of the managing partner
    • Membership of bodies such as the INSS, the INPP, the FEC and COPEMECO
    • Organizational chart of the applicant firm

  3. On-site inspection

    The investor follows up the on-site inspection.

Approval fees

Public road transport approval
US$1,000
Public rail transport approval

Operating license in the maritime sector

  • A construction permit, a sine qua non for any vessel built in the DRC;
  • For a vessel bought abroad, a deregistration certificate from the country of origin;
  • A sea letter.

After inspection and approval, the following documents are issued:

  • Certificate of seaworthiness
  • Tonnage (gauging) certificate
  • Certificate of registration

Operating license in aviation

  1. Apply to the Minister

    Send a license application letter to the Minister of Transport and Communication Routes.

  2. Civil Aviation Authority assessment

    Send a copy to the Director General of the Civil Aviation Authority (AAC), which assesses the applicant’s legal, financial and technical guarantees, at the investor’s expense.

  3. Payment to the Treasury

    Pay the Treasury after AAC approval, based on the findings of the initial inquiry.

  4. Operating license granted

  5. Air operator certificate

    Submit the airline certificate application to the Civil Aviation Authority. The certificate is issued only to holders of a valid operating license.

Legal framework

Road transport

Law 78-022 of August 30, 1978 on the new Highway Code, in force since July 25, 1979, regulates traffic on public roads by pedestrians, vehicles, draft, pack and saddle animals, and livestock.

Rail transport

The railway sector is a State monopoly in the DRC. The principal legislation governing railway transport is the decree of the Sovereign King of October 10, 1908 on the railway police, which coordinated earlier measures on the railway police, as amended by orders 82/TP of September 18, 1928 and 41/06 of February 13, 1954 on the same subject. Railway activities are also governed by the rules of the International Union of Railways, the Union of African Railways and the public rail transport companies themselves.

Air transport

Air transport is governed by Law No. 10/014 of December 31, 2010 on International Civil Aviation, which aims, among other things, to give the country adequate and efficient aviation legislation.

Maritime transport

Ordinance-Law 66-98 of March 14, 1966 establishing the Maritime Navigation Code is the basic legislative framework for maritime transport in the DRC.

River and lake transport

Ordinance-Law 66-96 of March 14, 1966 establishing the River and Lake Navigation Code is the backbone of the sector’s legislation.
