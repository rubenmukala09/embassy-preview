Hydrocarbons in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

Oil and gas discoveries in the east of the country give the DRC the second-largest crude oil reserves in Central and Southern Africa. These reserves lie mainly in the four major lakes on the borders with Tanzania, Burundi, Rwanda and Uganda.

Natural gas and methane

Along with large, recently identified oil fields, the DRC may hold as much as 30 billion cubic meters of methane and natural gas in its three major petroleum deposits. Lake Kivu, bordering Rwanda and Burundi, has nearly 60 billion cubic meters of methane dissolved in its waters. Beyond this estimated stock, the lake generates as much as 250,000 cubic meters of methane each year.

Sedimentary basins

The DRC has three sedimentary basins:

  1. The Coastal Basin, located in Kongo Central, which extends offshore to the Congo River estuary.
  2. The Central Basin.
  3. The Albertine and Tanganyika Grabens, which extend from the Uganda–DRC border to the southern tip of Lake Tanganyika on the Zambia–DRC border.

Potential

The DRC holds 6% of Africa’s oil stock, notably in:

  • the Coastal Basin: 5,992 km², in Kongo Central province;
  • the Central Basin: a sedimentary basin of 800,000 km², with 32 blocks not yet allocated;
  • the western branch of the East African Rift:
    • the Albertine Graben: 5 blocks, 1 open for exploration;
    • the Tanganyika Graben: 11 blocks;
    • Lake Kivu: open for methane gas exploration;
    • Lake Upemba.

Leading sub-sectors

  • Refined petroleum products, including gasoline, aviation fuel and kerosene
  • Petroleum-based lubricants
  • Oil refining operations
  • Biofuel production

Recent reforms

Investment opportunities

  • Certification of oil and gas reserves.
  • Prospecting and exploration of the sedimentary basins.
  • Exploitation of Lake Kivu methane gas.
  • Construction of a pipeline crossing the basin.
  • Construction of storage facilities.
  • Construction of a refinery.
  • Production of road bitumen in Kongo Central province.
  • Storage, transportation and pipelines.

Legal framework

Previous legislation

Until recently, the DRC’s oil and gas sector was governed by Ordinance-Law No. 81-013 establishing general legislation on mines and hydrocarbons, as amended by Ordinance-Law No. 82-039 of November 5, 1982 and Law No. 86-008 of December 27, 1986 (the Mines and Hydrocarbons Law). Since 2002, however, the mining sector has been governed by a separate Mining Code, which made many provisions of the Mines and Hydrocarbons Law obsolete.

Hydrocarbons Law (Law No. 15/012 of August 1, 2015) and Regulations (Decree No. 16/010 of April 19, 2016)

After several years of deliberation on reforming its oil and gas legislation, the DRC adopted Law No. 15/012 of August 1, 2015 establishing a general regime for hydrocarbons (the Hydrocarbons Law). The reform was completed by Decree No. 16/010 of April 19, 2016 establishing the hydrocarbons regulations (the Regulations), which among other things sets out the applicable fiscal regime in more detail. The main provisions of the Hydrocarbons Law and Regulations for oil companies engaged in upstream activities are summarized below.

Main provisions for upstream activities

Ownership
The State owns all national hydrocarbon resources up to the point of export.
Award of rights
Petroleum exploration or exploitation rights may be granted to local or foreign legal entities selected through competitive bidding, on technical and financial criteria set by the Minister of Hydrocarbons and approved by the Council of Ministers. Rights are granted under a petroleum contract. Unlike the former Mines and Hydrocarbons Law, the Hydrocarbons Law no longer provides for concessions.
Foreign companies
Foreign entities must form local subsidiaries under DRC law to carry out petroleum exploration or exploitation.
Exploration rights
Valid for 3 years (4 years for areas with difficult geological or access conditions), renewable twice for the same period. Under the Mines and Hydrocarbons Law, exploration rights lasted at most 8 years.
Exploitation rights
Granted for up to 20 years, renewable once for up to 10 years (comparable to concessions under the Mines and Hydrocarbons Law). Holders must start work within 12 months of the approval of the development and production plan.
State participation
The State-owned oil company (presumably COHYDRO, Congolaise des Hydrocarbures, created by Decree-Law No. 245 of August 9, 1999) is entitled to a minimum 20% stake in upstream petroleum activities, under an association contract with third parties that does not create a separate legal entity.
