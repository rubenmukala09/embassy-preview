Housing in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The DRC’s largest cities are growing by about 4.7% a year. The national housing deficit is estimated at 3,945,555 homes, which means building 263,039 homes a year, more than half of them in Kinshasa.

The Government’s vision

The Government’s vision for the sector has three parts:

  1. Reorganizing the housing sector: institutional reform and capacity building.
  2. Improving housing: land development policy and supervision of real estate.
  3. Raising funds for housing to eliminate the housing shortage, especially in poor urban areas.

Housing development

In the past five years, housing supply and private housing development have increased slightly. In 2011, for example, the central government launched the Cité Kin Oasis housing project, which delivered 1,000 social homes in Bandalungwa, Kinshasa.

Most housing development projects are in the capital, Kinshasa, including Cité du Fleuve, Cité de l’Espoir, Cité Belle Vie and Cité Moderne. Another is in Fungurume, a mining district in the newly created province of Lualaba.

Procedures

Construction permit
12 procedures and 122 days, at a cost of about 6% of the property value
Property registration
7 procedures and 44 days

There is room for further progress through a recent initiative, carried out by a local firm, Congo Check, to digitize land titles and titling procedures. It could both implement and improve current land administration systems by increasing transparency.

Legal framework

Land Law (Law No. 73-021 of July 20, 1973)

Law No. 73-021 of July 20, 1973 on the general regime of property, land tenure, real estate and security interests sets precise standards governing the purchase, sale and lease of land and concessions. It frames the decisions of sellers, lessees and buyers on the procedure for every transaction leading to the issue of highly secure real estate titles by the Congolese State.

Bakajika Law (June 7, 1966)

The Bakajika Law states that “the soil and subsoil belong to the State.” It was intended to bring order to the land sector.

Building permits (Ministerial Order No. Cab/MINA/TUHITPR/007/2013 of June 26, 2013)

This order regulates the issue of building permits in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Anyone planning a property development, urban project, building or structure of any kind in durable materials, built to professional standards, anywhere in the country must first obtain a building permit from the relevant urban planning and housing administration.

Transfer of land and property rights (Circular Note No. 005/CAB/MIN/AFF FUNC/2013 of June 12, 2013)

This circular note sets the procedure and time limit for the transfer of land and property rights.
