Banking and finance in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The banking, insurance and microfinance sectors aim to create specialized financial institutions and to make existing banks operate more efficiently. All of these sectors are fully open to competition.

The financial system

The DRC’s banking system comprises the Central Bank of Congo (BCC), 18 commercial banks, savings and credit cooperatives, microfinance institutions, money transfer services and one development bank, SOFIDE. A postal checking system and several credit cooperatives also exist. Citibank-DRC, a wholly owned subsidiary of Citigroup, is the only U.S. bank in the DRC.

Recent reforms

  • Enactment of the law liberalizing the insurance sector.
  • Adoption of a more liberal banking law in line with international standards.
  • Enactment of the leasing law in 2015.
  • Enactment of the law on microfinance institutions in 2011.
  • Enactment of the new foreign exchange regulations in 2014.

Investment opportunities

  • Creating specialized banks: a development bank, a settlement bank, an agricultural bank and others.
  • Creating a structured financial market.
  • Creating microfinance institutions in rural areas, where more than 70% of the population lives, excluded from the formal banking system.
  • Setting up leasing companies.
  • Creating insurance, reinsurance and health insurance companies, and private insurance intermediaries.

Licensing a bank

A banking institution must be approved by the Central Bank of Congo. The conditions for approval are as follows.

1. Legal framework

Law No. 003/2002 of February 2, 2002 on the activity and supervision of credit institutions.

2. Conditions for the company

  • Be a duly incorporated limited company (société anonyme) under OHADA law.
  • Show a minimum subscribed and paid-up capital equivalent in Congolese francs to US$15,000,000 (fifteen million dollars).
  • Meet general or local economic needs.

Requirements, including the minimum capital, are set by the Central Bank of Congo and may change. Confirm the current conditions with the Central Bank of Congo or with ANAPI before applying.

3. Conditions for officers

No one may establish, manage or control a bank in any capacity if he or she has been:

  • convicted of an offense under Law No. 003/2002 of February 2, 2002 or the foreign exchange regulations;
  • declared bankrupt and not rehabilitated, even if the bankruptcy was opened in another country;
  • sentenced in the Congo or abroad to a prison term of at least three months, even suspended, as perpetrator of, accomplice in or for attempting any of the following offenses:
    • counterfeiting currency;
    • forgery or falsification of government securities, banknotes, bonds or interest coupons;
    • forgery or falsification of seals, stamps, dies and trademarks;
    • forgery and use of forged documents;
    • corruption of public officials or extortion;
    • theft, extortion, embezzlement or breach of trust, fraud or receiving stolen goods;
    • fraudulent bankruptcy, or fictitious circulation of commercial paper;
    • money laundering.
  • convicted of political crimes, or of offenses that the law treats as equivalent to those listed above;
  • involved in the administration, management or day-to-day management of a credit institution whose compulsory dissolution was ordered or which was declared bankrupt.

4. Documents to submit to the Central Bank of Congo

  • The letter applying for approval, addressed to the Governor of the Central Bank of Congo;
  • The resolution of the company’s constituent general meeting;
  • Registration in the Trade and Personal Property Credit Register;
  • The national identification number;
  • The resolution of the extraordinary general meeting of each corporate shareholder of the new bank, authorizing it to take a stake in the capital of the future bank;
  • The original notarized articles of association of the new company;
  • Proof of payment of the required minimum capital: a certificate from a local or foreign bank confirming that it holds that capital on deposit for the proposed bank;
  • The list of shareholders;
  • The composition of the Board of Directors and of the bank’s Management Committee, with the curriculum vitae and criminal records of their members and of individual shareholders;
  • Certified financial statements for the last three financial years of corporate shareholders;
  • The implementation and organization plans;
  • A statement of the general policy the bank intends to follow;
  • A feasibility study with activity forecasts, backed by projected balance sheets and operating accounts over at least five years, showing that the project is profitable;
  • Details of the technical and financial resources the credit institution intends to deploy.

For banks with foreign capital, approval also requires the Central Bank of Congo to obtain the approval of the supervisory authority in the home country of the parent company or main promoters. Through its banking supervision body, the Central Bank of Congo may also request any other information that helps it reach a decision.

Procedures published by ANAPI

The National Agency for Investment Promotion (ANAPI) publishes step-by-step approval procedures, in French, for micro-credit and microfinance institutions and for banks:

  • Approval of a micro-credit enterprise or a microfinance company (anapi.cd, in French)
  • Creating a bank (anapi.cd, in French)
