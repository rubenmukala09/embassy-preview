Investment laws in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

Within the framework of the Government’s new economic policy, the country’s new Investment Code is designed to encourage and secure investment, alongside other laws that support business development. The Investment Code simplifies the approval procedure for granting customs and tax benefits.

Investment Code: key points

Important points include:

  • The DRC’s membership of OHADA (Organization for the Harmonization of Business Law in Africa).
  • The exclusion of nationalization and expropriation.
  • Freedom to transfer dividends and other investment income abroad.
  • Settlement of disputes under the ICSID Convention (International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes, known in French as CIRDI), based in Washington, D.C.
  • A one-stop shop for investment: ANAPI.
  • The removal of certain formalities for setting up a company, such as visa authentication, the certificate of non-State employment and the criminal record.
  • A significant reduction in the cost of obtaining the new Trade Register.
  • Publication of company charters in the Official Gazette (Journal officiel) via the internet, to save time.
  • Many notary offices set up throughout the country.
  • Mechanisms to secure investments (see “Investment protection” and “International guarantees” below).

Support for investors: ANAPI

To give investors effective support that helps guarantee their competitiveness, the State has set up a public body entirely dedicated to serving them: ANAPI, the National Agency for Investment Promotion.

ANAPI offers investors a range of services before, during and after they set up in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The most important are:

  • Help with setting up companies.
  • Timely information, provided directly on the ANAPI website (anapi.cd), in communication materials such as its newsletter, and on multimedia platforms such as social media.
  • Granting of tax and customs benefits under the Investment Code.
  • Obtaining an entry or settlement visa.
  • Finding developed or undeveloped land and premises (with water and electricity).
  • Building partnerships with both public and private operators.
  • Access to finance.
  • Obtaining the required sector licenses.
  • A welcome at the airport or port.
  • Organization of the investor’s stay (appointments with authorities or potential partners).
  • Transportation from the airport or port to downtown and back.
  • Accompaniment in Kinshasa and in the provinces.
ANAPI provides all the services investors need. Visit the ANAPI website

Legal basis for Investment Code benefits

The procedure for approval to the benefits of the Investment Code is based on the following texts:

  • Law No. 004/2002 of February 21, 2002 on the Investment Code.
  • Decree No. 009/33 of August 8, 2009 on the statutes, organization and functioning of ANAPI.
  • Decree No. 12/046 of November 1, 2012 on measures implementing the Investment Code.
  • The project approval procedures manual adopted by the Approval Council in July 2013.

Approval procedure

The promoter sends an application for approval to the National Agency for Investment Promotion (ANAPI), with a file containing the following:

  1. A copy of the investment project, following the model attached to the Investment Code.
  2. The list of equipment and materials to be imported, in electronic form.
  3. Evidence of the company’s legal existence (notarized articles of association, RCCM number, tax number) and other useful documents (title deeds, partnership contract, etc.).
  4. Proof of payment of the filing fee to the bank: US $1,000 for large companies and US $500 for small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) projects, together with the application (for a file being processed).

ANAPI publishes its approval procedure and application file in French: Approval procedure on the ANAPI website

How the application is processed

  1. ANAPI analyzes the project

    ANAPI receives investment projects once the filing fee has been paid to the bank, analyzes them and gives its opinion on their approval under the general regime of the Investment Code.

  2. The Approval Council validates

    The Approval Council examines the projects analyzed by ANAPI and validates them in minutes, after any amendments.

  3. ANAPI informs the promoter

    ANAPI informs the promoter that the Approval Council has approved or rejected the project.

  4. Ministers sign the decree

    The draft inter-ministerial decrees approved by the Approval Council are submitted for signature to the Minister of Planning and the Minister of Finance.

  5. Decree delivered within 30 days

    ANAPI hands the inter-ministerial approval decree to the promoter within 30 days at most from the date the application was submitted to ANAPI. After this deadline, approval is deemed granted.

The financial authorities and ANAPI must then let the investor enjoy all the benefits of the Investment Code on presentation of the file submission receipt signed by the Managing Director of ANAPI or his delegate, and of the list of goods to be imported signed by the delegates of ANAPI, the DGDA (customs), the DGI (tax authority) and the DGRAD.

The customs and tax benefits, their duration and the eligibility conditions are set out on the page Taxes related to investment.

Investment protection mechanisms

  • The Constitution and the laws of the country provide that the property, private and collective, of any person in the Democratic Republic of the Congo is sacred. This protects owners, except in cases provided for by law, particularly court sentences for certain offenses.
  • The Investment Code excludes the nationalization and expropriation of investments made in the country, except in extreme cases, for a public purpose and against fair and just compensation paid in advance.
  • The law provides for and guarantees equal treatment of all national and foreign investors. No discrimination may be made between a Congolese investor and a foreign investor.
  • The Investment Code guarantees respect for investors’ acquired rights: no later law or regulation may restrict benefits previously granted to an investor in a legal and regular manner, unless the Government has amended the law.
  • The law guarantees the freedom to transfer abroad, without restriction, revenues, dividends and other earnings from investments made in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.
  • In any investment dispute, the law gives priority to amicable settlement or arbitration.

International guarantees

The Democratic Republic of the Congo is a member of the following international instruments and institutions:

ICSID
Member since April 29, 1970 of the Convention of March 18, 1965 on the Settlement of Investment Disputes between States and Nationals of Other States. This instrument provides dispute-resolution mechanisms suited to business needs, focused on amicable settlement and arbitration.
MIGA
Member of the Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency, a branch of the World Bank Group that insures international investors against non-commercial risks such as insurgencies, civil wars, popular uprisings and looting.
ACA
Member of the Agency for Trade Insurance in Africa (ACA), based in Nairobi, Kenya. This international institution facilitates, encourages and develops insurance and reinsurance, guarantees and other financial instruments for trade, investment and other productive activities in Africa. Its guarantees may cover political and commercial risks, or non-commercial risks.
OHADA
Full member of the Organization for the Harmonization of Business Law in Africa since July 13, 2012; this modern African business law has been in force in the DRC since September 12, 2012. OHADA law simplifies business procedures and increases legal and judicial security for investors through mechanisms that include the Common Court of Justice and Arbitration (CCJA), a simplified procedure for setting up a business, debt collection, and a regular procedure for informing and warning shareholders.
Bilateral investment agreements
Agreements for the promotion and reciprocal protection of investments signed with several partner countries, including the United States of America, France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Germany, Greece, Sweden, Italy, Switzerland, China, South Korea, the Republic of South Africa, Jordan and Egypt.
