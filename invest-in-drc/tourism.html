Tourism in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

In tourism and travel, a myriad of opportunities exist in the rehabilitation and construction of hotels, restaurants and other tourism infrastructure. The DRC’s unique attractions, including wildlife reserves, indigenous cultures and geological wonders, make it a perfect country for those who seek to connect with nature and humankind.

Twelve percent of the country is made up of protected areas, including 7 national parks and 57 reserves with great ecotourism potential. The DRC offers a wide range of attractions across its provinces, from the seaside to safaris and cultural sites, and caters to business and leisure travelers alike.

Investment opportunities

  • Rehabilitation and construction of hotels, restaurants, protected areas and other tourism infrastructure.
  • Design of new tourist sites.
  • Modernization of national parks, with opportunities for joint management.
  • Development of ecotourism.
  • Construction of a hotel and a tourist site in Virunga National Park.
  • Rehabilitation of the Eala botanical garden in Equateur.
  • Creation of river boat tours and tourist transport.
  • Creation of a marina, lakeside transport and hiking.
  • Installation of a cable car in the Virunga Mountains and at the volcanic site, with accommodation and catering.
  • Creation of seaside resorts with beaches, shoreline access and camping equipment.

Targeted investment projects depend on public-private partnership and require feasibility studies.

Goals of the tourism master plan

In 2013 the DRC revised its tourism master plan, which is projected over 15 years, with the following targets:

International tourists
1,146,962
Revenue
US$17,300,000, estimated at 10% of the value of the Congolese GDP in 2012

To generate these flows, the plan targets the following areas of intervention:

  1. Promotion and preservation of the flagship animal species: rehabilitation of national parks, creation of ecological niches and rehabilitation of access roads;
  2. Promotion of green tourism: environmental conservation;
  3. Development of reserves and natural sites: creation of recreation sites and amusement parks;
  4. Tourist transport services (air, road, river and lake);
  5. Creation of places to stay: hotels, hospitality facilities and tourist entertainment; with decentralization, the new provinces will need luxury hotels;
  6. Creation of travel agency and tourist office services;
  7. Support for tourism training and promotion (guides, website editing, online travel agencies, design and production of promotional materials).

Discovering the DRC

Nature and wildlife

  • 25 million hectares, or 10.47% of the country, are protected areas.
  • 7 national parks and 57 reserves and hunting areas, 5 of which are on the UNESCO World Heritage List: Virunga, Kahuzi-Biega, Garamba and Salonga national parks and the Okapi Wildlife Reserve.
  • 4 emblematic species: the mountain gorilla, the okapi, the bonobo (pygmy chimpanzee) and the Congo peafowl.
  • 450 species of mammals, 300 species of reptiles, 200 species of amphibians and more than 1,000 species of birds.
  • The Congo rainforest is the only place in the world where all three gorilla subspecies are found: the mountain gorilla, the lowland gorilla and the eastern lowland gorilla.
  • Bonobos are found only in the Congo rainforest; they are the closest relatives of human beings.
  • The okapi looks like a cross between a giraffe and a horse and is often called the forest giraffe. It is brown, with white-striped legs.
  • A variety of ecosystems covering nearly 145 million hectares: the second-largest block of tropical forest in the world after the Amazon, and one of the planet’s biodiversity reserves.

Landscapes, culture and people

  • Natural sites: the Congo River, the Atlantic coast (37 km), impressive waterfalls, lakes and their shores, and the mountainous areas of the East.
  • Built heritage: heritage monuments and religious buildings.
  • Culture: a rich mix of cultures and traditions from around 450 ethnic groups, art markets and historic sites.
  • A population of 70 million: the Congolese themselves are a prime target for tourism promoters, especially young people looking for recreation.

Achievements

  • The peace restoration process is in its final stages.
  • Renovation of the botanical and zoological gardens of Kinshasa through public-private partnership.
  • Modernization of 4 major airports: Kinshasa, Lubumbashi, Goma and Kisangani.
  • Support for the approval of about a hundred tourism investment projects, including 85 hotel projects.
  • Increased capacity through the rehabilitation and modernization of some Congolese heritage hotels through public-private partnership, including three 5-star hotels: the Grand Hotel Kinshasa and the Fleuve Congo Hotel in Kinshasa, and the Grand Karavia Hotel in Lubumbashi.
  • Creation of a national airline.
  • Reduction to four of the number of public services operating at border posts: migration management, the hygiene department, the Congolese Control Authority and the customs authority.
  • Opening the country to major events and international forums (forums, conferences, sports events).
  • Rehabilitation and construction of historic sites and creation of public leisure spaces.

Why invest in tourism?

Positioned in the heart of Africa, the DRC lies on the Equator, at the crossroads of Southern, Central and Eastern Africa. Connections between its provinces make it possible to organize inter-provincial and intra-regional circuits to visit the 11 provinces, which border 9 African countries.

Tourism products shared with neighboring countries and the extension of regional destinations are opportunities to strengthen existing markets and to create new tourism markets in the Great Lakes region and in SADC, COMESA and ECCAS, to which the DRC belongs.

The DRC continues to attract tourists, with growing travel flows dominated by young people (over 60% of the population) and the middle class, the Congolese diaspora, international business visitors and culture enthusiasts.

Legal framework

The revival of tourism is among the DRC Government’s priorities. The sector, which grew out of the environment portfolio, has been given its own ministry, which will shortly be provided with framework legislation now being drafted.

New reform

A very attractive tourism code is in the process of being adopted.

Legal references are given as published by the Embassy. Before investing, check the texts currently in force with the National Agency for Investment Promotion (ANAPI): anapi.cd
