Mining in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The DRC’s mineral wealth spans 1,100 different minerals, and every province has its own. The sector has attracted subsidiaries of several large multinationals, and the Government is working to strengthen governance, transparency and the traceability of public revenues.

Multinationals in the DRC

The sector has attracted subsidiaries of several large multinationals. Success stories include companies such as:

  • Tenke Fungurume Mining (Freeport-McMoRan)
  • Kolwezi Copper Company (Kinross-EGMF)
  • Kibali Gold Mining
  • Mutanda
  • Ashanti Goldfields Kilo
  • Twangiza Mining

Vision and reforms

The Government of the DRC aims to further improve governance and transparency in the management of the mining sector, as well as the traceability of public revenues. To this end, it will:

  1. Evaluate, revise and popularize Act No. 007/2002 of July 11, 2002 on the Mining Code;
  2. Set up a national geological department;
  3. Evaluate the titles already granted in order to improve the mining cadastre records.

Reforms

  • Promulgation of a new Mining Code in 2002, currently being revised.
  • Creation of a Mining Registry (Cadastre Minier, CAMI) in 2002.

Leading sub-sectors

  • Copper and nickel ore mining
  • Gold ore mining
  • Cobalt ore mining
  • Mining equipment leasing and financing
  • Power generation and transmission

Investment opportunities

  • Setting up processing units to transform mining products.
  • Setting up diamond cutting, gold refining, and smelting of tin ores and their associated minerals.
  • Geological research zones (ZRG) for exploration work and the development of deposits, with convincing indications of cassiterite, wolfram, coltan, gold, diamond and copper.
  • Installation of cable manufacturing industries.

Prospecting is open to investors through a certificate issued by the Mining Registry. The certificate does not authorize mining and does not guarantee that rights over the prospected areas will be granted later.

Characteristics of Congolese minerals

  • The Banalia deposits have a high iron content of 65%, with a low silica (SiO₂) content.
  • Katanga’s iron ores (magnetite, hematite, martite and goethite) contain between 54% and 66% iron and between 3% and 21% silica, without many impurities.
  • Chromium and nickel have average contents of 1.45% and 3.8% Cr.
  • The lead vanadate deposit has an average ore content of 25–30% lead and 5–6% vanadium.
  • Bauxite is found at Sumbi and Sanzala, with a content of 48% Al.

How to invest in mining

There are two ways to invest in the mining sector:

  1. A partnership with State portfolio companies; or
  2. A partnership with other companies that already hold mining concessions.

Mining titles are requested from the Mining Registry (CAMI) under the conditions and procedure below: www.cami.cd

Conditions for an operating license

The operating license is granted only if the applicant:

  • Proves the existence of an economically exploitable deposit by presenting a feasibility study, together with a technical plan for the development, construction and operation of the mine;
  • Proves that it has the financial resources needed to carry out the project, through a financing plan for the development, construction and operation of the mine and for the rehabilitation of the site at closure. The plan specifies each type of financing, the sources of funding and the reasons they are likely to be available;
  • Transfers 5% of the shares in the applicant company’s capital to the State. These shares are unencumbered and cannot be diluted.

Who is eligible for mining and quarry rights

  • Any natural person of Congolese nationality, and any legal entity under Congolese law with its headquarters in the national territory and whose corporate purpose is mining.
  • Any natural person of foreign nationality and any legal entity under foreign law.
  • Any scientific research organization.

Natural and legal persons under foreign law must act through a mining and quarry agent.

Application file

The application is admissible when it includes:

  • A duly completed and signed application form, filed with the Mining Registry (www.cami.cd)
  • The applicant’s identity documents or, for a legal entity, its articles of association
  • A certificate of financial capacity issued by CAMI, or other evidence of financial capacity
  • Geographic data for the requested perimeter
  • Proof of payment of the application filing fee
  • Any other documents required for the type of right requested

How the application is processed

  1. Cadastral examination

    The registry examination covers:

    • Verification of eligibility
    • Verification of financial capacity
    • Indicative plotting of the perimeter on the mining cadastral map
    • Verification of possible overlaps between the requested perimeter and existing third-party rights
    • Compliance with the “first come, first served” principle

  2. Technical examination

  3. Environmental examination

  4. Inter-ministerial order

How mining rights are granted

The law provides for two procedures for granting mining rights:

  1. As a general rule, on application;
  2. Exceptionally, by tender (open or restricted).

Legal framework

The mining sector in the DRC is regulated by Law No. 007/2002 of July 11, 2002 on the Mining Code and Decree No. 038/2003 of March 26, 2003 on the Mining Regulations.

The current Congolese Mining Code was enacted by the Congolese Congress on July 11, 2002. Its implementing measures are set out in the Mining Regulations of March 26, 2003.

Classification of deposits

The Code classifies mineral deposits as mines or quarries. It specifies that the President of the Republic may declassify a mineral substance or reclassify it as a quarry product, and vice versa.

Objectives of the Mining Code

  • Create a win-win framework for all;
  • Develop and encourage infrastructure for all;
  • Develop the DRC’s human capital;
  • Develop local processing capacity;
  • Develop the DRC as a destination for foreign capital and technology;
  • Encourage the country’s immediate development rather than speculation.

Revision of the Mining Code

The new Mining Code will aim, in particular, to:

  1. Enhance the Government’s control over the mining sector;
  2. Further regulate the social and environmental responsibility of mining companies;
  3. Incorporate the latest changes in the Congolese administrative context, such as the introduction of VAT into the Congolese tax system;
  4. Address financial and accounting manipulations (such as high interest rates charged by the operator’s affiliates acting as lenders) used by mining companies to channel profits to their foreign shareholders’ groups.

Stability guarantee

The current Mining Code provides for a 10-year stability guarantee: the rights attached to mining rights that are valid on the date the Act amending the Mining Code is promulgated will remain unchanged and vested for 10 years. This period starts from:

  1. The entry into force of the Act amending the Mining Code, for mining rights that are valid at that time; or
  2. The date a mining right is granted, provided it was granted under a valid research permit that existed when the Act amending the Mining Code entered into force.

Provincial reorganization

From a political point of view, the redefinition of the DRC’s provinces is also likely to have some impact on operators. Since July 2015, when the Government gave effect to the increase in the number of provinces from 11 to 26 mandated by the 2006 Constitution, the main copper-producing region, Katanga, has been split into four separate provinces. The 400-kilometer (249-mile) journey from Kolwezi to the Zambian border now crosses two provinces, Haut-Katanga and Lualaba, instead of one.

OHADA and the one-stop shop

At the regional level, the DRC joined the Organization for the Harmonization of Business Law in Africa (OHADA) in July 2012. OHADA law is of particular interest to mining companies, as it mainly covers commercial, corporate, loan-guarantee, accounting and arbitration law. It entered into force in the DRC on September 12, 2012. In addition, a one-stop shop for business start-ups (Guichet Unique) was recently set up and shows encouraging development.

Institutions

The Mining Cadastre receives applications for mining rights, grants mining rights and keeps records of them, among other functions. The DRC has also created a national committee for the transparency initiative in the management of extractive industries. Regulations are issued by the Ministry of Mines, which supervises mining activities at the national level.

Legal references are given as published by the Embassy. Before investing, check the texts currently in force with the Mining Registry (CAMI) or the National Agency for Investment Promotion (ANAPI): anapi.cd
