Forestry in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The Congo Basin holds more than 45% of Africa’s equatorial forest and 6% of the world’s tropical forest reserves. The Congolese forest contains roughly 1,000 tree species and some 11,000 plant species, in rainforest so dense that in places only 1% of the sunlight reaches the ground.

Overview

Congo Basin
An estimated 530 million hectares of land, of which 200 to 300 million hectares are forest and woodland, with an area of approximately 1,232,000 m²

More than 45% of Africa’s equatorial forest and 6% of the world’s tropical forest reserves

Unique to the DRC

  • Lianas, which look a lot like vines, grow up to three thousand feet (914 meters) long.
  • Teak trees are common in the Congo rainforest and grow up to 50 meters (164 feet) high.

Recent reforms

  • Enactment of a new Forestry Code in 2002.
  • Withdrawal of the moratorium.
  • Fight against illegal logging and the illegal timber trade.
  • Continuation of the forest management planning process, to guarantee sustainable national management.

Investment opportunities

  • Investment in agroforestry.
  • Development of pulp and paper industries.
  • Industrial wood processing: sawing, cutting, plywood, beams and rafters.
  • Production of wooden poles for power lines.
  • Production and transport of logs.
  • 16 plots of land available, returned to the State by their former holders after the conversion process.

Logging permit procedure

  1. Identify the concession

    The investor identifies the concession to be logged.

  2. Apply for a logging permit

    The investor submits the logging permit application, with the required information, to the Provincial Coordination of the Ministry of Environment, Wildlife and Tourism. The application must include:

    • the identity of the applicant (investor);
    • the logging location;
    • the area requested;
    • a sketch map at 1:20,000 and/or 1:200,000 (territory map);
    • the list of species with the corresponding volumes;
    • the list of equipment;
    • quarterly statements, for former operators.

  3. Provincial review

    If the file is compliant, the Provincial Coordination issues a favorable opinion and forwards the file to the Ministry of Environment.

  4. Forest Management Department

    The Ministry sends the file to the Forest Management Department (DGF) for processing. The DGF analyzes the file and gives its opinion.

  5. Liquidation notes

    If its opinion is favorable, the DGF issues the liquidation notes, with a copy of the application, and sends them to the National Forestry Fund (FFN).

  6. Debit and collection notes

    The National Forestry Fund issues a debit note to the para-fiscal revenue administration (DGRAD), which then issues the collection notes.

  7. Pay duties and taxes

    The investor pays the duties and taxes and presents proof of payment to the DGF.

  8. Permit signature

    The DGF prepares the draft logging permit and sends it to the Minister for signature, with the proof of payment.

Moratorium. Since 2006, the Congolese Government has suspended the issuance of new permits to investors, to allow old titles (supply guarantees) to be converted into new forest concessions. Once the moratorium ends, forest concessions will be awarded by tender.

Legal framework

Forestry Code (Law No. 011/2002 of August 29, 2002) and Decree No. 05/116 of October 24, 2005

This law governs the exploitation of forest resources throughout the country. It promotes the rational and sustainable management of natural forest resources, to increase their contribution to the economic, social and cultural development of present generations while preserving forest ecosystems and biodiversity for future generations.
