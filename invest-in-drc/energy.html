Energy in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The DRC’s immense energy potential includes non-renewable resources such as oil, natural gas and uranium, and renewable sources including hydroelectric, biomass, solar, wind and geothermal power. The Government’s vision is to raise the level of electricity service to 32% by 2030.

Overview

The Congo River, the second-largest river in the world, has a basin that straddles the Equator. It provides an energy potential estimated at 100,000 MW, spread across 780 sites in 145 territories and 76,000 villages. This represents about 37% of Africa’s total potential and about 6% of the world’s.

Hydroelectric potential
About 100,000 MW across 780 sites: about 37% of Africa’s potential and 6% of the world’s
Installed capacity
About 2,516 MW in total, of which about 2,416 MW (96%) belongs to SNEL, the national electricity company
Main source
Hydroelectric power, generated mainly by the Inga I and Inga II dams in Kongo Central province
Current production
Only 6,000 to 7,000 GWh
Other resources
Oil, natural gas and uranium; biomass, solar, wind and geothermal power

Vision for the sector

  • Raise the electricity supply rate from 9% to 32% by 2030.
  • Build energy highways along the following routes:
    1. Inga site → Gabon → Cameroon → Nigeria → Mali
    2. Inga site → Angola → Namibia → Botswana → South Africa
    3. Inga site → Central African Republic → Chad → Libya
    4. Inga site → South Africa → Sudan → Egypt
    5. Inga site → Malawi → Zambia → Zimbabwe → Lesotho

Leading sub-sectors

  • Electric power generation and solar energy
  • Transmission line construction
  • Last-mile power distribution infrastructure
  • Mobile power generators
  • Hydroelectric turbine construction and maintenance

Recent reforms

  • Enactment of the Electricity Act in June 2014, which liberalized the sector.
  • Establishment of an electricity regulatory authority.
  • Transformation of SNEL (the national electricity company) and REGIDESO (the water utility) into commercial companies.
  • A projected 59% increase in the country’s level of electrification by 2030.

Investment opportunities

  • Construction of hydroelectric dams.
  • Construction of four pan-African energy highways.
  • Construction of the hydroelectric plants and Inga III schemes listed below.
  • Rehabilitation and modernization of units G13 and G16 of Inga I and unit G24 of Inga II.
  • 217 hydroelectric sites identified for the construction of power plants.
  • Production potential estimated at 44,000 MW.
  • Transmission, distribution and marketing potential.

Procedures

A. Becoming a self-producer of electricity

  • To build a hydroelectric power plant for your own use, obtain a license from the Ministry of Energy and Water Resources. Email: rdcministerenergie@yahoo.fr
  • To sell electricity, apply to the same Ministry.
  • In either case, the promoter can rely on the support of ANAPI, the National Agency for Investment Promotion, which will advocate on the promoter’s behalf with the Ministry concerned.

B. Partnership procedure

Financing a project
Contact the Ministry of Energy and Water Resources or SNEL, the public electricity company, directly to sign a memorandum of understanding (MoU). An MoU may be considered after the discussions held with the Ministry of Energy and SNEL.
Implementing a project
Be registered as a company established in the DRC, and obtain the approval of the Ministries of Energy and Public Works.

C. Connecting to the grid at medium voltage

A medium-voltage (MV) connection requires four steps and takes 18 days in total.

  1. Open the file (3 days)

    • The applicant obtains the application form from SNEL’s research department in charge of connecting private cabins, or from the SNEL website (www.snel-rdc.com).
    • The applicant submits the complete file: the form, civil engineering plans and wiring diagrams.

  2. Technical studies and approval (7 days)

    • SNEL reviews the file for the new medium-voltage/low-voltage (MV/LV) cabin against the standard plans and diagrams, and assesses whether it can be connected to the network.
    • If its opinion is favorable, SNEL issues a quotation at the applicant’s expense; if not, SNEL notifies the applicant of the refusal.

  3. Technical inspection (1 day)

    • The applicant pays SNEL’s quotation and notifies SNEL that construction of the cabin is complete.
    • SNEL checks that the work matches the approved drawings and diagrams, and that the connection equipment meets the required technical specifications.

  4. Signature and commissioning (7 days)

    • Payment of the subscription fee.
    • Signature of the energy supply contract.
    • Connection of the MV/LV cabin to the network.
    • Installation and adjustment of the metering and automation systems.
    • Commissioning of the cabin.

Total time required: 18 days.

Legal framework

Electricity Act (Law No. 14/011 of June 17, 2014)

Law No. 14/011 of June 17, 2014 regulates the energy sector in the DRC. It provides for:

  • the effective liberalization of the sector;
  • the promotion and balanced development of supply in urban, peri-urban and rural areas;
  • meeting the electricity needs of every category of user with a quality supply;
  • guaranteed fair competition between operators, and protection of users’ rights.

The law applies to the production, transmission, distribution, import, export and sale of electricity by any operator.
