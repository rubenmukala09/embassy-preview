Infrastructure in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

There are numerous infrastructure construction opportunities for American firms, and most projects are structured as public-private partnerships. In March 2016, the Ministry of Infrastructure and Public Works identified more than $6 billion in transportation and utility infrastructure construction and rehabilitation projects over the following six years.

The Government’s objectives are to modernize railroad infrastructure and airport safety and security equipment, and to maintain, develop and mark the country’s waterways.

Potential

Roads
A road network of 145,000 km, of which only 3,000 km are paved
Railways
A 5,033 km railroad network to be fully rehabilitated
Waterways
A 16,238 km maritime, river and lake network to mark, dredge and operate
Airports
270 airport platforms to rebuild
Ports
2 international seaports to modernize, and several ports across the country to equip and dredge
Economic hubs
Roads to interconnect the country’s economic hubs

Infrastructure assets

The DRC has several assets in the infrastructure sector, including:

Roads
238,935 km of roads
Railways
5,033 km of railways
Airports and airstrips
500 landing strips and 270 airports, including 101 open to public traffic, 164 private aerodromes and 5 international airports; 25 have paved runways
Waterways
16,238 km of waterways
Bridges
2,513 km of bridges (1,965 bridges)
Energy
89 hydroelectric plants and other renewable resources

Leading sub-sectors

  • Health care infrastructure
  • Road infrastructure
  • Electricity generation and transmission infrastructure
  • Water and sewage infrastructure
  • Lodging
  • Educational infrastructure

Investment opportunities

  • Construction of a deep-water port at Banana.
  • Construction of railroads, including the Banana–Matadi–Kinshasa–Ilebo railway.
  • Modernization of the SNCC and SCTP railroads.
  • Modernization and equipment of the seaports of Matadi and Boma.
  • Modernization of SCTP ports across the country.

New reform

A bill on public-private partnership would allow the private sector to invest in areas previously reserved exclusively for the public sector (see the legal framework below).

Achievements

  • The Bukanga Lonzo agro-industrial park.
  • Rehabilitation of the runway at Goma airport.
  • Rehabilitation of the Route des Poids Lourds in Kinshasa.
  • Rehabilitation of the Kinshasa–Kikwit road.
  • Construction of the bridge over the Loange River connecting the provinces of Bandundu and Kasai-Occidental.
  • Modernization of the runway and terminal building at N’djili Airport.
  • Construction of the Hôpital du Cinquantenaire in Kinshasa.
  • Construction of the Ngaliema water catchment plant.
  • Rehabilitation and modernization of Boulevard Lumumba.
  • Construction of the Katende hydroelectric plant in Kasai-Occidental.
  • Construction of the Kakobola hydroelectric plant in Bandundu.
  • Rehabilitation of several universities across the country.
  • Construction of the Cité du Fleuve.
  • Construction of housing under the OASIS project in Kinshasa (Bandalungwa).
  • Rehabilitation of the Lubumbashi–Kasumbalesa road, among others.

Legal framework

Law No. 14/011 of June 17, 2014

This law liberalizes the energy sector, opening it to private operators. It applies to production, transmission, distribution, import, export and marketing. It does not apply to plants with an installed capacity of 50 kW or less for non-commercial use, to signal or speech distribution facilities, to scientific research facilities or to State security facilities.

Public-private partnership bill

A bill on public-private partnership is awaiting consideration by the National Assembly. It provides for the following forms:

  • Service contract
  • Management and maintenance contract (leasing)
  • Rental and construction–management
  • Build–transfer–management
  • Other forms

Legal references are given as published by the Embassy. Before investing, check the texts currently in force with the National Agency for Investment Promotion (ANAPI): anapi.cd
