In brief

A nation rebuilding and growing

As the second-largest country on the African continent, the DRC is emerging as a stable and promising leader in Africa, with improved security, a growing economy and a commitment to democratic governance.

Since a peace accord signed in 2002 ended years of civil conflict, the Congolese people have taken critical steps: forming a unified military, laying new foundations for economic progress and pursuing their democratic aspirations, guided by the Constitution.

Rich in both human and natural resources, the DRC has adopted policies that are generating strong economic growth and attracting the interest and engagement of international investors. Its entrepreneurial workforce has shown resilience and determination.

To support this progress, the nation’s infrastructure is being rebuilt: new roads, schools and hospitals under construction create jobs and stimulate further commercial activity.

The Constitution

Adopted by referendum in 2005 and promulgated on February 18, 2006, the Constitution guarantees freedom of speech and religion.

Emerging from conflict under the leadership of President Joseph Kabila, the Government set out to build a new Congo, with an inclusive, transparent and democratic government able to provide a better future for all citizens.

People and culture

One Republic, many languages

French is the official language. Kikongo, Lingala, Kiswahili and Tshiluba are national languages, spoken alongside the many regional and community languages that carry the country’s histories and creativity.

In 2021 UNESCO inscribed Congolese rumba on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Growth

The World Bank expected growth to average 5% in 2017–2018, up from 2.7% in 2016, on stronger commodity prices and expanding agriculture and services. Other reports cited by the Embassy put real GDP growth at 2.4% in 2016 and 2.8% in 2017, rising to 3% in 2018 on stronger mining production, led by copper and cobalt, and stabilizing external accounts.

Mining and the deficit

Low commodity prices in 2015–2017 weighed on the profitability of mining companies and other sectors, and on overall growth. Growing mining exports in 2017 and 2018 were expected to narrow the deficit to a projected 2.1% of GDP by 2018. Growth remained held back by the challenge of developing adequate infrastructure across the country.

Inflation and interest rates

Headline inflation eased from 39.6% year on year in January to 36.3% in February. It had accelerated sharply in 2016, after low commodity prices and insufficient US dollar inflows weakened the Congolese franc. In response, the Banque Centrale du Congo raised its base rate from 7% to 14% in February 2017.

For current figures, consult the World Bank’s DRC overview and the Banque Centrale du Congo.

Natural heritage

Five natural World Heritage sites

UNESCO lists five natural properties in the DRC. Together they protect rainforest, savanna and volcanic landscapes, and species found nowhere else, such as the okapi and the bonobo.

The DRC holds a large portion of the Congo Basin, the second-largest tropical forest in the world. Its protected habitats include Garamba National Park and Virunga National Park, the world’s oldest nature preserve of its kind.

  • Virunga National Park
  • Garamba National Park

Gorillas of the eastern forests

Eastern lowland gorillas, also known as Grauer’s gorillas, live only in the eastern DRC, including Kahuzi-Biega National Park.

Mountain gorillas live in the Virunga massif, a chain of volcanoes shared by the DRC’s Virunga National Park and protected areas in Rwanda and Uganda, and in Uganda’s Bwindi forest.

Access to national parks and border areas can change at short notice. Before planning a visit, check current travel advice and the conditions published by the park authorities.
