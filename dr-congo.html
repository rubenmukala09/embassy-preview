Geography

The Congo on the map

At the heart of Africa, the Democratic Republic of the Congo straddles the Equator and shares land borders with nine countries. Its major cities reach across the nation, from Kinshasa on the Congo River in the west to Lubumbashi in the mineral-rich south-east.

Official priority sectors

Build where the world’s next systems begin.

Explore concise sector briefs based on national investment priorities. Open any card to read the full opportunity.

01Cu · Co

Mining & value chains

Copper, cobalt and other strategic minerals can support responsible local processing and higher-value manufacturing.

Read investment brief +

From resources to industrial capability

The DRC is central to global copper and cobalt supply. The strongest investment case extends beyond extraction into geological services, traceability, cleaner processing, engineering, logistics, worker skills and local manufacturing. Projects that strengthen transparent supply chains and create durable value inside the country are positioned to serve both African and international markets.

Reference: ANAPI investor guidance and national diversification priorities.

02SOIL

Agriculture & agro-industry

Exceptional agronomic diversity supports food production, processing and regional supply chains throughout the year.

Read investment brief +

Fertile land, varied climate, scalable value chains

ANAPI identifies maize, cassava, rice and soy among crops with strong industrial development potential, alongside coffee, cacao, tea, palm oil, livestock, fisheries and aquaculture. Opportunity exists across seed systems, irrigation, storage, cold chains, processing, packaging and market access. Investment should combine productivity with responsible land use and resilient farming practices.

Reference: ANAPI agriculture sector guidance.

0342 GW

Energy & hydropower

The Congo River system gives the country a globally significant platform for reliable, lower-carbon power.

Read investment brief +

Powering households, industry and regional growth

The World Bank estimates the Inga site’s hydropower potential at around 42,000 MW. Realising this opportunity requires phased investment in generation, transmission, distribution, off-grid systems, governance and community benefit. Reliable electricity can unlock mineral processing, digital services, manufacturing and improved public services across the country.

Reference: World Bank Grand Inga development programme factsheet, updated June 2025.

04LINK

Infrastructure & logistics

Road, rail, river, aviation and urban systems can connect productive regions to people and markets.

Read investment brief +

Connecting a country of continental scale

The DRC’s geography makes integrated logistics essential. Modern corridors can combine rehabilitated roads and rail with inland waterways, border facilities, renewable power and digital tracking. Well-structured public-private investment can reduce transport costs while improving access to farms, industrial zones, cities and regional trade routes.

Reference: ANAPI investor guide and national development priorities.

055G+

Digital & telecommunications

Connectivity, cloud services, fintech and digital public infrastructure can serve one of Africa’s largest populations.

Read investment brief +

A connected platform for a young market

Investment opportunities span national fibre, resilient data infrastructure, mobile coverage, cybersecurity, digital identity, payments and enterprise software. The most durable models will widen affordable access, build local technical capacity and connect public services and businesses beyond major urban centres.

Reference: ANAPI investor guidance identifies ICT among national investment opportunities.

06VISIT

Tourism & hospitality

Five UNESCO-listed natural properties anchor an extraordinary conservation and visitor economy.

Read investment brief +

High-value travel rooted in conservation

Opportunities include responsible lodging, domestic aviation, guide training, cultural programming and park-adjacent services. Development must respect current security guidance, protected-area management and community participation. Premium tourism works best when conservation revenue and local enterprise grow together.

Reference: UNESCO World Heritage Centre and ANAPI diversification priorities.

01

National parks

From Virunga’s volcanic landscapes to the vast forests of Salonga, the DRC’s protected areas hold exceptional biodiversity.

Discover the parks +

UNESCO lists five natural properties in the DRC. Each landscape has its own conservation story, visitor conditions and management needs; travellers should consult official park and security guidance before planning.

02

Congo River

A transport artery, ecological system and cultural horizon connecting the interior with Kinshasa and the Atlantic corridor.

Read the river story +

The Congo is one of the world’s great river systems. Its flow supports communities, trade, fisheries, biodiversity and major hydropower potential. River experiences should be arranged with established local operators and current safety information.

03

Gorillas & wildlife

Mountain and eastern lowland gorillas share the country with bonobos, okapi, forest elephants and remarkable birdlife.

Read the wildlife story +

Wildlife tourism is most meaningful when it funds conservation and respects strict viewing protocols. Protected habitats in eastern DRC are globally important, but access conditions can change and must be checked with official authorities.

04

Culture & cuisine

Rumba, fashion, visual art and regional cuisines make Congolese culture one of Africa’s most influential creative forces.

Enter the culture story +

Congolese rumba was inscribed by UNESCO in 2021 as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Contemporary culture moves from orchestras and dance floors to galleries, design studios and kitchens serving dishes shaped by cassava, plantain, fish, greens, spices and regional traditions.

Kinshasa · music, art & design

Culture & heritage

Modern, expressive, unmistakably Congolese.

Congolese rumba connects generations and cities. Fashion turns colour and tailoring into a language of confidence. Visual artists, filmmakers and designers continually reshape how the country sees itself and how the world sees the DRC.

Music & rumba +

Inscribed by UNESCO in 2021, Congolese rumba is performed by professional and community orchestras and remains central to celebration, memory and cultural entrepreneurship.

Art & fashion +

Kinshasa’s creative ecosystem moves between fine art, photography, performance, street style, tailoring and contemporary design.

Cuisine +

Regional tables draw on cassava, plantain, fish, beans, greens, peanuts, spices and seasonal produce, with contemporary chefs presenting Congolese ingredients in new formats.
