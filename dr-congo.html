The country

The DRC, seen from every horizon

Rainforest and river in the north and west, volcanic highlands and great lakes in the east, savanna and plateaux in the south: the country spans some of Africa’s most varied landscapes.

Illustrative photography.

Geography

At the heart of Africa

Africa’s second-largest country by area, the DRC straddles the Equator and reaches the Atlantic Ocean at the mouth of the Congo River.

Capital
Kinshasa
Area
About 2.34 million km²
Neighbouring countries
Nine: Angola, Burundi, Central African Republic, Republic of the Congo, Rwanda, South Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda and Zambia
Cities on the map
Kinshasa, Lubumbashi, Mbuji-Mayi, Kisangani, Goma

People and culture

One Republic, many languages

French is the official language. Kikongo, Lingala, Kiswahili and Tshiluba are national languages, spoken alongside the many regional and community languages that carry the country’s histories and creativity.

In 2021 UNESCO inscribed Congolese rumba on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Invest in the DRC

Priority sectors for investment

The Embassy’s investment guide covers ten priority sectors, with ANAPI support and the investment code. Four of them are introduced here.

Open the investment guide

  • Mining and value chains

    Responsible extraction, mineral processing, traceability and local suppliers around strategic resources such as copper and cobalt.

  • Energy

    Hydroelectricity, transmission and off-grid power. The World Bank estimates the Inga site’s hydropower potential at around 42,000 MW.

  • Agriculture and agribusiness

    Arable land and diverse growing regions, with opportunities in crops, fisheries, processing and agricultural logistics.

  • Infrastructure

    Transport corridors, public works and water systems that connect production centres to cities and regional markets.

Natural heritage

Five natural World Heritage sites

UNESCO lists five natural properties in the DRC. Together they protect rainforest, savanna and volcanic landscapes, and species found nowhere else, such as the okapi and the bonobo.

  • Virunga National Park
  • Garamba National Park
  • Kahuzi-Biega National Park
  • Salonga National Park
  • Okapi Wildlife Reserve

The DRC on the UNESCO World Heritage List

Gorillas of the eastern forests

Eastern lowland gorillas live only in the eastern DRC, including Kahuzi-Biega National Park. Mountain gorillas, shown below, live in the Virunga massif, which Virunga National Park shares with Rwanda and Uganda, and in Uganda’s Bwindi forest.

Access to national parks and border areas can change at short notice. Before planning a visit, check current travel advice and the conditions published by the park authorities.
