Internal process · Demonstration

Consular case workflow

How a consular file moves through the Embassy, from the moment it is handed in at the counter to the moment it is collected or posted back. The stages, turnaround targets and counter hours below follow the Embassy’s published guidance.

Internal tool

Proposal: staff case management

A proposed internal tool for the Consular Section: one shared view of every file handed in, from the counter to collection, with each file measured against the published turnaround for its service.

At a glance

Who would use it
Consular Section staff only. It is not a public service.
What it shows
Where each file is, which desk holds it, what is still missing and how long it has been open.
Status
Proposal. It is not part of the Embassy’s current systems.
Applicants today
Files are handed in and collected in person, or sent by post. There is no online case tracking.

Working model

Sample queue

Choose a desk to see the files it holds. Open a file to see its checklist and history, and move it on to the next stage.

Working model with invented sample files, identified by reference only. Nothing is saved, sent or recorded; reloading the page restores the sample.

The working model needs JavaScript to run.

Reference

Published turnaround

The times the model measures each file against, as published by the Embassy.

Published turnaround by service
ServiceTurnaround
PassportAbout 90 days, produced in Kinshasa
Visa, U.S. citizens1–2 business days
Visa, Congolese nationals1 business day
Visa, diplomatic and officialSame day, within 24 hours
Visa, all other travellers2 business days
Tenant-Lieu and legalizationA few business days

Times run from receipt of a complete file and payment. In the model, a file waiting on the applicant is paused until the missing document arrives.
